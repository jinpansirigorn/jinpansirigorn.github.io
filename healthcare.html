The question

Can patient characteristics reveal meaningful differences in readmission risk — and can unsupervised segmentation identify cohorts that may warrant more targeted follow-up?

High-risk vs. lower-risk patients

HIGH RISK22.1%30-day readmission
LOWER RISK14.5%30-day readmission

The difference was statistically significant in the submitted analysis (p = 0.00117), suggesting elevated readmission among patients classified with higher comorbidity burden.

Quantifying uncertainty

1,000 bootstrap samplesEstimated overall readmission at ~14.8%, with a 95% interval of 13.9%–15.6%.

Patient segmentation

K-means produced three relatively balanced patient segments. One cluster showed a 20.1% readmission rate and an average Charlson index of 2.57, versus 12.5% and 11.4% readmission in the other two clusters.

Cluster 020.1% readmission · higher comorbidity · candidate for closer follow-up.
Cluster 112.5% readmission · lower-risk indicators.
Cluster 211.4% readmission · lower comorbidity with somewhat higher blood pressure.

Business / clinical implication

Prioritize limited follow-up resources

Segmentation can help hospitals identify cohorts that may benefit from additional monitoring after discharge, while uncertainty estimates keep planning grounded in the variability of the data.

Important limitation

This case uses synthetic EHR data for educational analysis. The findings are analytical demonstrations, not clinical guidance, and would require validation on appropriate real-world data before operational use.
