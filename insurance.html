Insurance Risk Analytics

Predicting claim probability and expected loss for risk-based pricing — while keeping model decisions interpretable.

THE QUESTION

How can an insurer price risk more accurately without creating a black box?

Insurance pricing depends on expected loss. Charge too little and high-risk policies can become unprofitable; charge too much and lower-risk customers may leave. This project therefore had two linked goals: estimate expected loss and predict whether a policyholder will make a claim.

ANSWER IN ONE MINUTE

Separate claim occurrence from claim severity, then compare that structure against a Tweedie alternative.

The hurdle approach produced the best loss-cost error in our comparison, while LightGBM produced the strongest claim-status ROC-AUC. SHAP was then used to explain which features pushed claim probability up or down.

WHY THE PROBLEM IS DIFFERENT

Most policies never generate a claim.

About 89% of policies in the project had zero claims. That means a single ordinary regression has to model two different processes at once: whether a loss occurs and, conditional on a loss, how large it is.

MODEL STRATEGY

Frequency × severity gives the model a business interpretation.

The two-stage structure mirrors how expected insurance loss is naturally understood: first estimate the probability of a claim, then estimate severity for positive claims. A Tweedie GBM provided a strong single-stage benchmark.

03

MODEL EVIDENCE

Did the added structure improve prediction?

Yes, modestly. For loss cost, the hurdle model reduced cross-validated MSE versus the constant baseline and outperformed the tested Tweedie configuration. For claim status, LightGBM outperformed Random Forest and Logistic Regression.

EXPLAINABILITY

Accuracy was not the only requirement.

In a pricing context, a model that performs well but cannot be explained is harder to trust and govern. SHAP was used after model selection to understand the direction and relative importance of features rather than relying on a black-box score alone.

BUSINESS TAKEAWAY

Use prediction and interpretation together.

What the model can support

Risk segmentation, policy-level expected-loss estimates, and more informed pricing decisions within the same insurance context.

What it should not be used for

Direct transfer to life insurance. Auto-risk drivers such as vehicle, urban location, and driving history do not substitute for mortality and health information.
