The business problem

Insurance pricing depends on estimating expected losses accurately. Pricing too high can push lower-risk customers away; pricing too low can attract disproportionately risky policies. The analysis therefore addressed two linked questions: will a claim occur? and what loss should the insurer expect?

Why this is difficultAbout 89% of policies had no claim, making the target highly zero-inflated and poorly suited to a single ordinary regression approach.

Explainability

SHAP was used to move beyond model accuracy and identify which variables were driving predictions. The project highlighted premium, vehicle/driver characteristics, location, and policy-duration-related features among the important signals.

Business takeaway

Accuracy matters for pricing, but explainability matters for trust and governance. The final approach combined predictive performance with an interpretable view of the drivers behind claim risk.

What I would improve next

For a production setting, I would add temporal validation, explicit calibration analysis, fairness monitoring, and business-cost evaluation so model selection reflects not only statistical accuracy but also pricing consequences.
