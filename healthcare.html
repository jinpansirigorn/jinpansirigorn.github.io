Healthcare Readmission Analytics

Identifying patient groups with different 30-day readmission risk using inference, uncertainty estimation, and segmentation.

PythonHypothesis TestingBootstrapPCAK-means

THE QUESTION

Can we identify patient groups that face meaningfully different readmission risk?

The case used a synthetic EHR dataset modeled on U.S. adult patients, allowing realistic healthcare analytics without exposing real patient information. I combined patient and outcome data, engineered risk indicators, and used inference plus unsupervised learning to look for actionable differences.

ANSWER IN ONE MINUTE

Higher-comorbidity patients showed materially higher 30-day readmission, and clustering independently surfaced a higher-risk segment.

The useful business interpretation is not “predict every patient perfectly.” It is “identify where limited follow-up resources may deserve more attention.”

UNCERTAINTY

A point estimate is more useful when its uncertainty is visible.

Using 1,000 bootstrap samples, the overall readmission rate was estimated at about 14.8%, with a 95% interval of approximately 13.9%–15.6%. That frames the metric as a range rather than false precision.

SEGMENTATION

Clustering surfaced a second view of patient risk.

K-means produced three balanced patient segments. Cluster 0 combined the highest readmission rate with a substantially higher average Charlson comorbidity index, making it the clearest candidate for deeper clinical review.

BUSINESS / HEALTHCARE TAKEAWAY

Use segmentation to prioritize investigation, not to automate care decisions.

Potential use

Flag higher-risk cohorts for additional monitoring, discharge planning, or follow-up programs, then evaluate whether those interventions reduce readmissions.

Important limitation

The data is synthetic and the clustering is exploratory. A real deployment would require clinical validation, fairness review, prospective testing, and stronger causal evidence.
