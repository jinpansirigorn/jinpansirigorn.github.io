The business problem

TuneWorks had transactional data across customers, agents, entertainers, styles, and engagements but no in-house analytics function. The goal was to turn those linked tables into actionable insight for retention, productivity, and growth.

The finding that changed the analysis

Stated preference ≠ observed behavior

The highest-spending customer generated $25,585 in bookings, yet 87.5% of her bookings fell outside her recorded music preferences. Another customer had a 100% non-preferred booking rate.

Instead of treating this as a final answer, the analysis generated new hypotheses: were preferences stale, was preferred supply unavailable, or were price, availability, and popularity stronger purchase drivers?

SQL techniques used

LAG(startdate) OVER (
  PARTITION BY customerid
  ORDER BY startdate
)

Window functions measured booking intervals; CTEs combined customer spending and frequency; multi-table joins connected actual engagements with entertainer styles and customer preferences.

Business actions

RetentionDifferentiate offers for highest-value, high-frequency, and re-engagement customers.
MarketingDo not rely on stated genre preference alone when observed booking behavior says otherwise.
Agent managementReview productivity and customer-concentration risk alongside salary and commission.
Data collectionAdd behavioral and availability data to explain why customers choose specific bookings.
