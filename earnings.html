REGRESSION + INFERENCE

Graduate Outcomes & Earnings

An observational analysis of U.S. College Scorecard data asking whether institutional demographic and socioeconomic composition is associated with median graduate earnings after accounting for school characteristics.

1,565institutions in the final regression analysis
44.2%variation explained by the model (R²)
Associationnot causation — an explicit analytical boundary

The analytical challenge

Students self-select into universities, so a simple comparison between schools can confound demographic composition with selectivity, graduation success, cost, geography and other institutional characteristics. The project therefore framed the ideal randomized experiment first, then used multivariable regression as an observational approximation.

Modeling strategy

The multivariable OLS model controls for institutional characteristics so that each demographic coefficient is interpreted conditionally on the included controls. The model was jointly statistically significant and explained about 44% of cross-institution earnings variation.

The wide interval is itself informative: institutional characteristics capture systematic patterns, but local labor markets, program mix and individual career paths leave substantial unexplained variation.

Interpretation boundary

Do not read institution-level coefficients as individual effects. The project identifies controlled associations in observational data; it does not establish that changing a school's demographic composition would causally change an individual's earnings.
