AI PRODUCT STRATEGY

Polaris Career Copilot

Turning recruiting pain points into a connected AI-assisted journey from job fit to warm outreach.

User ResearchAI ProductPRDKPI DesignImplementation

THE USER PROBLEM

Students did not need another generic career tool. They needed the pieces to connect.

The concept was designed for the USC MSBA community. User research pointed to fragmented recruiting: unclear job fit, uncertainty about skill gaps, difficulty navigating a large alumni network, and time-consuming cold outreach.

PRODUCT IN ONE MINUTE

Right job. Right person. Right message.

Polaris turns a resume into a structured profile, scores job fit, exposes skill gaps, identifies relevant alumni, and helps draft personalized outreach.

FROM PAIN POINTS TO FEATURES

Each module answers a specific recruiting friction.

“Which jobs actually fit me?”

→ Job Fit Engine compares experience and skills with role requirements.

“What am I missing?”

→ Action Plan turns skill gaps into concrete next steps.

“Who should I talk to?”

→ Outreach Mapping prioritizes relevant alumni and warmer paths.

“What should I say?”

→ Email Copilot drafts personalized outreach using context and purpose.

END-TO-END JOURNEY

The product is a workflow, not a chatbot.

01

Resume → AI profile

Structure background, experience, skills, and goals.

02

Profile → job fit

Score alignment and make gaps visible.

03

Fit → warm outreach

Find relevant alumni and generate a contextual message.

MEASUREMENT

Success should move from usage to career outcomes.

Generated emails are not the goal. The measurement framework deliberately moves from leading indicators to the outcomes the program actually cares about.

ADOPTION

Are students using it?

Active users, repeat use, fit checks.

ACTION

Does it change behavior?

Skill actions, warm introductions, outreach activity.

OUTCOME

Does it improve recruiting?

Interviews, time to offer, placement, fit.

IMPLEMENTATION

Pilot before scaling.

The team proposed a small 20–30 student pilot, followed by broader Marshall rollout and deeper alumni integration. The purpose of the pilot is not only adoption — it is to generate behavioral evidence about whether matching and outreach features actually improve recruiting outcomes.
