REGRESSION + INFERENCE

Graduate Earnings Analysis

Using College Scorecard data to understand how raw relationships change after institutional controls — without overstating causality.

OLSInferenceCollege ScorecardObservational Data

THE QUESTION

Which institutional characteristics are associated with graduate earnings — and what changes after controls?

Using U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard data, the project examined median earnings ten years after entry. The analytical challenge was to distinguish simple correlations from conditional associations after accounting for institutional characteristics.

ANSWER IN ONE MINUTE

Institutional context explained meaningful variation, but not enough to support causal claims.

The final multivariable model had adjusted R² around 44%. The project explicitly treated demographic coefficients as institution-level associations, not individual effects.

WHY OBSERVATIONAL DATA MATTERS

Regression cannot recreate the experiment we actually want.

Ideal experiment

Randomly assign otherwise comparable students to different university environments, then observe later earnings.

What we actually have

Students self-select into institutions, so selection bias and omitted confounding limit causal interpretation.

The important lesson is not whether a coefficient is “good” or “bad.” It is that raw demographic correlations can change after controlling for institutional factors, and the remaining relationships still should not be interpreted as individual causal effects.
